## About This Study

### The Sale
IBM Z and LinuxONE require sellers to build deep technical credibility, translate infrastructure advantages into business outcomes, and navigate complex internal processes — across sales cycles that routinely run 9–12 months. At every stage, the same person is expected to be the relationship owner, technical translator, content producer, and process navigator simultaneously.

### The Problem
The knowledge sellers need lives in official IBM content, AI tools adopted independently, and the heads of experienced colleagues. None of these talk to each other. New sellers learn by interrupting experienced ones — there is no documented onboarding path for pricing, ordering, or partner workflows. Sellers in Japan translate documents for 6+ hours each. Horizon sellers in pharma and utilities have watched deals disengage because nothing in the kit speaks to their industry. The tribal knowledge that makes the best sellers effective lives in personal OneDrives and personal networks. When those people move on, it disappears.

### Why This Research Exists
LinuxONE is expanding into new markets — digital assets, sovereign cloud, AI infrastructure, white-space accounts with no prior IBM presence — where the existing enablement model was never designed to operate. The sellers entering these markets are newer, less networked, and facing buyer personas the Sales Kit has never addressed. The gap between what sellers need to close a deal and what the organisation currently provides is not a content gap alone — it is a **systems gap**. This research makes that gap legible so it can be closed deliberately.

## Key Themes

### Confirmed Themes (T-01 to T-10)

**T-01 · C5 — Sales Kit Discoverability & Access**
Sellers across all regions struggle to find information in Seismic. The core problem is not content absence but navigation and search inadequacy. Sellers need to already know where to look; Seismic search returns documents rather than answers. Competitive intelligence is entirely absent from Seismic. Content update alerts, when set up, never arrive. Matt Mondics (Americas) encounters dead/broken bookmarked links routinely and explicitly requests a maintained 'Sales Kit of Sales Kits' cross-kit master index. He frames Seismic as "an aggregator, not enablement."
*11 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-02 · C5 — Sales Kit Content Value**
Client Presentations & Key Deliverables is the universally highest-value section. The TLLB (Technical Library for LinuxONE) is a high-value technical reference. Nilanshu (India, 15yr veteran): "100% in all my wins." Moesha (Americas/Horizon): win stories and use cases are her top asset. Jacqueline (South Africa/Haven): client references are the #1 ask. Matt Mondics (Americas): most-used asset is the "What LinuxONE and LinuxMZ can do that x86 can't" deck; avoids one-pagers ("come off as really salesy"). **No TLLB exists for LinuxONE Emperor 5** — Matt manually created one by stripping ZOS content from the Z17 TLLB.
*11 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-03 · C5 — Content Quality & Currency**
Content gaps confirmed across 12 participants. Missing: IBM DB2 consolidation scenarios, data centre footprint parameters, business-outcome translation layer, banking-only win stories (deal-blocking for utilities/pharma), Haven-specific content, customer-facing 'under the hood' technical brief (gap between TLLB and client overview), TLLB for Emperor 5, IBM-endorsed multi-tool integration scenarios (OpenShift + Terraform + Ansible + IBM Infrastructure Management).
*12 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-04 · C5 — Information-Seeking Behaviour**
Sellers use a multi-source hierarchy: (1) personal saved files / bookmarks, (2) AI tools, (3) Seismic/TLLB, (4) colleagues/SMEs. AI tools are rapidly displacing Seismic as the first query destination. Nilanshu supplements with Forbes and third-party reporting for competitive credibility. Jacqueline runs a multi-platform AI stack (Watson Workshop + Ask Aura + Copilot + Finlistics). Matt Mondics maintains a personal browser bookmark folder of all Sales Kits and a personal OneDrive archive of hundreds of self-built decks.
*11 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-05 · C5 — Sales Kit Usage Patterns**
Three consistent patterns: (1) heavy use during onboarding/ramp, (2) product-announcement spikes (especially DOAP), (3) local-copy plateau — sellers stop returning after initial period. Nilanshu: usage decayed from multiple times daily in first three months to ~once a month. Moesha: monthly Seismic cadence + OneDrive storage. Matt Mondics: high-frequency user who clears his folder every 6 months and downloads fresh versions (active version management, not passive stale accumulation).
*12 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-06 · C5 — Enablement Process Friction**
Multiple undocumented, friction-heavy steps with no formal guidance. Formal pricing cycle: 7–10 days. PO loading: 3 tiers. LinuxONE has substantially fewer YourLearnings modules than Z hardware/software. SME access acutely isolated for Africa/MEA — nearly all IBM LinuxONE SMEs based in Europe. **Expert Labs: only 2 people nationally in the US who can implement LinuxONE, booked through 2028** — deals stall because implementation capacity doesn't exist. Hands-on access to live LinuxONE infrastructure unavailable to most sellers.
*12 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-07 · C5 — Collaboration & Knowledge Sharing**
Institutional knowledge held informally, not systematically captured. Ashish relies on weekly APAC huddles. Nilanshu has 15 years of built-up network. Moesha onboarded by shadowing Rishi. Matt Mondics (Americas) doesn't seek tribal knowledge — he *produces* it. He built the IT optimisation demo now used informally by multiple sellers. None of his content has ever entered Seismic. Karan (India) independently built a Quantum Safety deck with no contribution pathway. No official capture mechanism exists.
*11 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-08 · C5 — AI Tool Adoption & Language Impact**
AI tools (Copilot, Bob, Claude, Gemini NotebookLM, Watson Workshop, Ask Aura, Finlistics) used by 12 of 13 participants as workarounds for Seismic limitations. Moesha used Copilot to synthesise a custom 3-day LinuxONE crash course for her own onboarding. Matt Mondics built a custom IBM Design System prompt loaded into Claude — produces brand-compliant presentations without AI hallmarks, applies explicit privacy guardrails ("I'm very, very clear about not putting any specific customer names in my prompts to Claude"). Assembled the Morgan Stanley deck from TLLB + IT Economics + Claude-processed Z17 TLLB content in a single session. Ashish is the only participant with limited AI adoption.
*13 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-09 · C4 — Content Organisation & Information Architecture**
Seismic IA navigable for launch-trigger users but opaque for newcomers or lateral search. Nilanshu's top improvement: "make it interactive." Jacqueline proposes tile-based redesign. Matt Mondics praises the 2025 four-focus-area structure (cloud native, data serving, secure, scalable) as the best structural improvement he has seen, and requests a 'Sales Kit of Sales Kits' — a single maintained Seismic page linking to all LinuxONE and Z sales plays.
*9 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**T-10 · C5 — Unmet Needs & Missing Capabilities**
Highest-cited gaps: competitive analysis tool (quick side-by-side with competitors); customer footprint intelligence tool (identify Oracle/VMware accounts due for server refresh); sales workflow automation; IBM software compatibility tracking tool. Additional: non-banking use case library (utilities, pharma, retail); YourLearnings content parity for LinuxONE; internal Z vs LinuxONE disambiguation; customer-facing technical architecture brief; 'Sales Kit of Sales Kits'; IBM-endorsed multi-tool integration story; hardware-level LinuxONE access in Tech Zone (LPAR, HMC, partition weights); expanded Expert Labs implementation capacity; seller-to-official-kit content contribution pathway.
*12 participants · EMEA, APAC, Americas*

**ET-02 (Sales Cycle Misalignment) — Detail:** Suresh and Nilanshu independently confirmed actual LinuxONE sales cycles run 9–12 months in India, while IBM expects 6 months. CRM data: one ICICI Bank opportunity spent 64 days in qualify and 179 days in design — over 243 days before entering the propose stage. This is a metrics/incentive problem, not a content problem.

**ET-11 (Shadow Content Library) — Detail:** Matt Mondics maintains hundreds of self-built presentations in a personal OneDrive. His IT optimisation demo circulates informally between sellers — was never in Seismic. Karan (India) built a Quantum Safety deck with no contribution pathway. Moesha built her "What is LinuxONE" deck from scratch. Three independent participants, three regions, three roles — all converge on the same workaround: create privately, use privately, share informally. Institutional asset value is lost every time a seller builds privately instead of contributing to the official kit.

## Seller Journey Map

The journey spans **6 stages** with a structural mismatch: ~270-day actual cycle vs 90-day quarterly targets.

### Stage 1 — Target & Qualify 🟠 HIGH FRICTION · Critical Gap
- **What sellers do:** Identify opportunities; understand customer stack (Oracle, VMware); cold calling; partner leverage
- **Enablement tool:** None — ET-05 (gap)
- **Workaround:** Cold calling, partner networks (TCS, Wipro), CXO relationships, AI tools to research customer tech stack from public signals
- **Evidence from:** Karan, Suresh, Nilanshu, Ashish (all APAC/India)

**Sub-stage 1b — Horizon Prospecting (Americas) 🟠 HIGH FRICTION · Critical Gap**
Moesha Malik (Americas/Horizon) exclusively targets accounts with NO existing Z or LinuxONE presence. Clients come from AI/data science, analytics, compliance, pharma, utilities — none infrastructure-aware. Entire Sales Kit is built for clients with infrastructure experience. No "What is LinuxONE for a layman" entry-point deck existed — Moesha built her own. Banking-only win stories caused two separate deal disengagements in the same week (utilities + pharma).

**Sub-stage 1c — Haven Prospecting (EMEA/South Africa) 🟠 HIGH FRICTION · Critical Gap**
Jacqueline Coelho (South Africa) sells Digital Asset Haven — launched October 2025. Clients are in crypto/digital assets, nascent SA regulatory environment. Key gap: no competitor intelligence for Thales (named competitor, active bid lost), no Africa-specific client references, no Haven-specific enablement bundle. Only participant who actively uses and shares video (podcast-format Simon Fulu videos). Uses Finlistics (third-party commercial data platform) for SA market intelligence.

**Sub-stage 1d — Enterprise Technical Briefing (Americas) 🟠 HIGH FRICTION · Critical Gap**
Matt Mondics (Americas) faces top-to-top enterprise conversations: "We want to understand how this thing works under the hood. WHY it has these benefits." No customer-facing 'under the hood' technical brief exists. TLLB is too detailed and ZOS-heavy; client overview is too high-level. No mid-tier format exists. He assembled the Morgan Stanley briefing on-demand from TLLB + IT Economics + Claude-processed Z17 TLLB. No TLLB exists for LinuxONE Emperor 5. Tech Zone cannot provide hardware-level access (HMC, LPAR weights, partition management). Expert Labs: 2 people nationally, booked through 2028.

### Stage 2 — Prepare & Research 🟡 MEDIUM FRICTION · Partial Coverage
- AI replaces Seismic for conceptual Q&A. TLLB downloaded and kept offline. Moesha used Copilot to synthesise a 3-day LinuxONE crash course — novel AI-driven onboarding pattern.

### Stage 3 — Build Proposal 🔴 CRITICAL FRICTION · Critical Gap
- Non-English markets: 3-step process (find → translate → customise) vs 1-step for English speakers
- Japan: 6+ hours per document
- India: half-day to 2–3 days per proposal (Nilanshu — highest estimate in study)
- DOAP kits require same-day delivery at announcement
- **Workaround:** Copilot/AI translation with manual QA; prior-gen Japanese decks as translation base

### Stage 4 — Customer Engagement 🟢 LOW FRICTION · Functional
- Videos blocked by customer security filters in India; IKE 3D demo used for hardware visualisation
- Technical partner handles deep Q&A; videos only effective at events

### Stage 5 — Configure & Price 🔴 CRITICAL FRICTION
- E-Config + ePricer + worldwide approval for discounts + 3-level PO loading = multiple days added to close
- Formal pricing cycle: 7–10 days
- Red Hat and Confluent cross-sell recognition gap — no incentive to bundle

### Stage 6 — Close & Fulfil 🔴 CRITICAL FRICTION · Critical Gap
- Engage Support tickets, FOE submissions, DPL checks, credit checks, contract localisation — **none documented**
- New sellers must ask colleagues for each step individually
- ISV certification chicken-and-egg: no certified software without opportunity, no opportunity without certified software
