# IBM Z & LinuxONE Seller Enablement — Problem Context

## Summary

Selling IBM Z and LinuxONE is a long-cycle, knowledge-intensive consultative sale. Sellers are operating without a coherent system. The knowledge they need lives across three disconnected places: official IBM content, AI tools they've adopted independently, and experienced colleagues they interrupt daily. None of it is designed around what sellers actually face in front of a customer.

---

## The Sale

Selling IBM Z and LinuxONE is fundamentally a **knowledge-intensive, long-cycle consultative sale**. Unlike transactional products, these platforms require sellers to:

- Build deep technical credibility
- Translate abstract infrastructure advantages into business outcomes
- Navigate complex internal IBM processes

Sales cycles routinely run **9–12 months**.

The journey spans six stages:

1. Identify and qualify an opportunity
2. Build a knowledge base
3. Construct a tailored proposal
4. Engage the customer technically
5. Configure and price the solution
6. Close and fulfill the order

At every stage, sellers are expected to be simultaneously the relationship owner, the technical translator, the content producer, and the process navigator.

---

## Core Problems

### 1. No coherent knowledge system

The knowledge sellers need lives in three separate places:

- **Official IBM content** (Sales Kit / Seismic)
- **AI tools** adopted independently by individual sellers
- **The heads of experienced colleagues**

None of these sources communicate with each other. None are reliably current. None are designed around the actual questions sellers face in front of a customer.

### 2. New sellers learn by interrupting experienced ones

There is no documented onboarding path for the procedural reality of the job — pricing approvals, order submission, contract localisation, partner workflows. Every new seller rediscovers these steps by asking someone who already knows. This is a daily tax on the organisation's most experienced people.

### 3. Content built for one customer type and one geography

- Win stories cover **banking only** — pharma and utilities deals disengage because nothing in the kit speaks to their industry
- Technical assets assume infrastructure familiarity
- Language support is an afterthought — sellers in **Japan** spend **6+ hours translating a single document**
- **Haven sellers in South Africa** are competing against Thales with no competitive intelligence on record

### 4. Tribal knowledge is the primary knowledge system — and it doesn't scale

The most effective sellers have spent years building personal networks, personal content libraries, and personal workarounds. That institutional capital lives in individual OneDrives and individual heads. When those people move on, it disappears.

---

## Why This Research Exists

IBM Z and LinuxONE are at an inflection point. LinuxONE in particular is expanding into genuinely new markets:

- Digital assets
- Sovereign cloud
- AI infrastructure
- White-space accounts with no prior IBM presence

The existing enablement model was never designed to operate in these markets. Sellers entering them are newer, less networked, and facing buyer personas and deal challenges the Sales Kit has never addressed.

The gap between what sellers need to close a deal and what the organisation currently provides is not a content gap alone — it is a **systems gap**.

> This research exists to make that gap legible so it can be closed deliberately, before it continues to cost deals.
